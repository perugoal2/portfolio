# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary: technical recruiters and hiring managers evaluating Peru N for a senior
software-architecture, cloud-engineering, or security-engineering role. They arrive
from LinkedIn, a job application, or a referral, often skimming many candidates in a
short window. Their job-to-be-done: within ~20 seconds, judge whether this person has
real depth (not buzzwords) and is worth advancing.

Secondary: prospective consulting clients, and engineering peers assessing technical
credibility. The site is optimized for hiring but must not alienate these audiences.

Context of use: mostly desktop during evaluation, but links are shared and opened on
mobile, so every surface must hold up small. Visitors are technically literate and
allergic to marketing fluff.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio that proves engineering depth through real artifacts — architecture
diagrams, threat models, decisions, trade-offs, and outcomes — rather than asserting
it through adjectives or skill bars. Success = a recruiter or hiring manager leaves
convinced enough to reach out or advance the candidate, and remembers the site among
a field of near-identical dev portfolios.

## Brand Personality

Three words: **distinctive, sharp, rigorous.**

- **Distinctive & memorable** — deliberately unlike the sea of dark-neon dev
  portfolios; a visitor should recall it later.
- **Sharp & modern** — current, technically ambitious, working at the edge of
  cloud / AI / security.
- **Precise & rigorous** — engineering discipline visible in the details: exact copy,
  clean contrast, considered spacing. The craft of the site mirrors the craft of the
  work.

Voice: confident but understated. Let the work carry the weight; no hype, no
exclamation, no growth-marketing cadence.

## Anti-references

- **Generic dark-neon dev site** — slate-950 + cyan glow, glassmorphism panels,
  radial gradient mesh. The default AI dev-portfolio look; explicitly rejected (the
  site was deliberately moved off it).
- **Over-designed / flashy** — animation-heavy, style-over-substance, decorative
  effects that obscure rather than reveal the work. Distinctiveness must never tip
  into spectacle.
- Also avoid: busy startup-landing marketing gradients/stock illustrations, and the
  bland styled-CV with no point of view.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the work, not adjectives.** Case studies lead with real architecture and
   threat-model artifacts, decisions, trade-offs, and outcomes — never skill meters
   or self-description.
2. **Distinctive, but disciplined.** A memorable point of view that never crosses into
   flashy. Every flourish must earn its place by clarifying the work.
3. **Rigor is the brand.** Precision in copy, contrast, spacing, and detail is itself
   the proof of engineering quality. Sloppiness anywhere undercuts the whole claim.
4. **Skimmable depth.** A recruiter must grasp the gist in 20 seconds; a peer must be
   able to go deep. Serve both — strong hierarchy up top, real substance underneath.
5. **Security-first framing.** Trust boundaries and threat thinking are part of every
   project story, not an appendix — consistent with the candidate's discipline.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target: **WCAG 2.1 AA.** Body text ≥4.5:1 contrast against its background; large text
≥3:1; visible keyboard focus on all interactive elements; full `prefers-reduced-motion`
alternatives for the page-load reveals and hover transitions. Dark UI, so verify the
muted "bone" text tokens against the ink background rather than assuming. Don't rely on
the cobalt accent alone to convey meaning (e.g. in recolored diagrams, keep
shape/label cues alongside color for color-vision-deficient viewers).
